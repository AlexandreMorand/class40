# Addendum: corrections and worked computations (Review Session IA)

## Corrections to two formulations in this fiche

**Correction 1: bank overdrafts.** An overdraft is part of cash and cash equivalents ONLY when it is repayable on demand AND forms an integral part of the entity's cash management. The total of cash and cash equivalents can then be negative. Otherwise the overdraft is a borrowing reported within financing activities. The "common mistakes" bullet on overdraft classification applies only when both conditions hold.

**Correction 2: interest and dividends under current IAS 7.** IAS 7 allows a free choice of classification, applied consistently from period to period. The course retains interest received and dividends received as investing inflows, interest paid as operating or financing, and dividends paid as operating or financing. IFRS 18 (mandatory from 2027) removes these choices: the indirect method starts from operating profit, dividends paid go to financing, and interest paid goes to financing in most cases.

## Worked computations in the review's format

**Worked example A: indirect method.** Profit before tax is €150,000 after depreciation of €30,000 and an €8,000 gain on disposal of equipment. Trade receivables increase by €20,000, inventory decreases by €5,000 and trade payables decrease by €4,000. Cash generated from operations = 150,000 + 30,000 − 8,000 − 20,000 + 5,000 − 4,000 = **€153,000**. The gain is removed because the full disposal proceeds are an investing inflow.

**Worked example B: direct method, receipts from customers.** Credit sales €700,000. Trade receivables €90,000 opening and €110,000 closing. Receipts = 90,000 + 700,000 − 110,000 = **€680,000**. The receivables increase means sales exceed collections.

**Worked example C: direct method, payments to suppliers.** Cost of sales €300,000. Inventory rises from €40,000 to €55,000. Trade payables fall from €30,000 to €25,000. Purchases = 300,000 + 55,000 − 40,000 = 315,000. Cash paid = purchases + opening payables − closing payables = 315,000 + 30,000 − 25,000 = **€320,000**.

**Worked example D: cash-equivalent boundary.** Moving €50,000 to a deposit repayable at seven days' notice, held to meet short-term cash commitments, convertible to a known amount without penalty and with insignificant value risk: the transfer is NOT a cash flow. It is a movement within cash and cash equivalents, so it does not appear in the statement.
